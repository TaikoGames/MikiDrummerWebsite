# Backlink outreach

Drafts to send. Nothing here has been sent — every one is your call.

The audit (`backlink-report.md`, regenerated monthly) crawled 30 sites across
69 pages: every band, venue, label and gear maker this site already features.
**Not one links back.** That is not a disaster, it is thirty unasked
questions, and every one of them has a real reason attached.

The rule behind all of these: never ask a stranger. Each message below opens
with something already done for the person receiving it, because a link
request with no relationship behind it is spam and gets treated as spam.

---

## 1. The two best asks: gear makers

Cymbal and stick companies run artist rosters, and those rosters link out to
the player's own site — it is the single most standard followed link in the
drumming world. You already play their gear and link to them from four pages
each. Nobody has asked.

No published address on file for either; both have a contact form, and for
these the better route is usually the artist-relations contact rather than
general enquiries.

## 6. Where the durable links are, beyond outreach

Outreach gets you tens of links. These are the categories that keep working:

**The tools are the linkbait, not the bio.** A free gig poster maker, QR
maker, merch mockup and cymbal designer are the things other people have a
reason to link to; a drummer's about page is not. Right now all eight are
buried in a section of `/bands.html` with no page of their own — there is no
`/tools/` index to link *to*. That is the single biggest structural gap: one
hub page, properly titled, gives every future mention one URL to point at
instead of a fragment link into an unrelated page.

**Profiles worth claiming** (most are nofollow, so they are for traffic and
discovery, not ranking — do not count them as wins): Bandcamp, Songkick,
BandsInTown, Discogs, Last.fm, and a Google Business Profile for the lessons
and session work, which is the one that genuinely moves local search.

**Session-work directories** are the exception — several pass followed links
and send real enquiries: SoundBetter and AirGigs both list remote drum
recording, which is exactly what `/services.html` sells.
