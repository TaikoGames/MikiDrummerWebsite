Free tools for bands and drummers

I'm Miki, a drummer in Vancouver. I kept needing small things that either did not exist or wanted forty dollars a month and an account, so I built them. There are eight now, they are all free, and there is nothing to sign up for.

Every one of them runs inside your browser. Your artwork, your audio and your photos never leave your machine, because there is no server here to send them to.

Putting a show on

The things you need between booking a gig and playing it.

Gig Poster Maker

Set the bill, the date and the venue and it lays out a poster. Pull a night straight off the Punk BC board and it fills itself in. Saves sized for an Instagram post, a story, or A4 print.

Open →

Merch Mockup Maker

Your logo on a shirt, hoodie, tote, cap, drum head or a pair of sticks, before you order a box of them. Photograph the actual blank and the artwork maps onto it in perspective, picking up the cloth's own folds.

Open →

QR Code Maker

A code for any link, with your logo in the middle if you want one. It warns you when the colours are too close to scan or the logo has grown big enough to break it — both limits measured by decoding real codes, not guessed.

Behind the kit, and after

Practice, playing, and the pile of audio that comes back afterwards. Some of these exist nowhere else, because the market for them is about four hundred people.

Gig Checklist

Breakables only, full kit, festival fly date, studio session or rehearsal — pick the kind of show and tick off what to pack. It remembers where you got to on your own device.

Open →

Click Track Maker

A metronome you can actually play to, and a click track you can take away — tempo, time signature and subdivision, then download it as a WAV for the session.

Open →

Cymbal Designer

Engrave or paint onto a virtual cymbal. Quarter symmetry repeats every stroke at 90°, 180° and 270°, so one gesture wraps the whole thing. Pick the size and finish and download it.

Open →

Drum Trigger Visualiser

Your kit on screen, lighting up as you play it. Drive it from an e-kit over MIDI or from a live mic feed, then set the background green or transparent and lay it over drum cam footage.

Open →

Razor Cut

Hand it a long recording and it splits it into separate clips wherever the audio goes quiet — takes, samples, voice memos, a whole rehearsal. Works offline once it has loaded.

Why they are free

I am a working drummer, not a software company. These came out of my own gigs and my own sessions, and putting them behind a login would have made them worse and earned roughly nothing.

If one of them saves you an afternoon, the useful thing you can do is link to it — that is genuinely how anyone else finds out these exist. If you need a drummer, the services page covers session work, remote recording and lessons, and the Punk BC board tracks punk and metal shows across British Columbia.
