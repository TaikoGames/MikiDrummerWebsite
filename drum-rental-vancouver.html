The snare shelf

The kit comes with a snare, but it is not the only one in the room. There is a shelf of them — steel and wood, deep and shallow — and they do not sound remotely alike. A snare is the one drum the whole room hears on every second beat, so it is the piece most worth getting right, and the cheapest thing to change if the first one is wrong.

  • Polished steel
  • Silver sparkle
  • Satin black
  • Wine-red glitter
  • Dark figured wood
  • Striped hardwood
  • Charcoal satin

Tell me the sound rather than the drum. Loud and cracky for a punk set, dry and low for a session, it has to cut through two guitars and a half-stack — that is enough to go on. I will bring two or three to the date and you pick at soundcheck, which costs nothing and settles an argument that otherwise runs all night.

Swapping the kit snare for one off the shelf does not change the price of the rental. A snare on its own is also a booking — plenty of drummers fly in with sticks and a pedal and need exactly one drum at the other end.

Who rents it

  • Touring bands flying in without a kit, or driving up with no room in the van.
  • Festival and multi-band bills that need a shared backline that will survive the night.
  • Studios and sessions wanting a kit tuned for the room and the song rather than a bigger kit that is not.
  • Video and photo shoots that need a kit that looks right on camera.
  • Players between kits, after a move or while something is being repaired.

Delivery, setup and tuning

A rental kit dropped at the loading door is half a job. I also set it up on the riser and tune it before doors, which on a four-band bill is the difference between a kit that sounds like a kit all night and one that sounds worse with every changeover. That is the same live setup and tuning I do for shows, and you can book it with the rental or without it.

Where it goes

Vancouver, Burnaby, New Westminster, Richmond, Surrey and North Vancouver as a normal run. Further up the valley or over to the Island is a question of timing rather than a no — ask with the date.

Questions

Can I see the kit first?

Yes — it is mapped out piece by piece on the drum map, and there is a walk-through of the room it lives in.

Can I choose the snare?

Yes, and it costs nothing to swap. Describe the sound you need and I will bring two or three off the shelf so you can pick at soundcheck.

Can I rent a snare on its own?

Yes. Drummers flying into Vancouver with sticks and a pedal need exactly one drum at the other end, and sessions often want a second snare to try against the one already in the room.

Do you rent to players you have not met?

Yes. Most rentals are bands passing through who I will never meet twice.

Can you play it instead?

If what you actually need is a drummer for the date rather than a kit, that is most of what I do — ask about both and I will tell you which is cheaper for you.
