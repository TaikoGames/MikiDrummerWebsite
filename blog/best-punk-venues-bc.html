The best punk & metal venues in BC

Where to see loud music in Vancouver, Victoria and beyond. What each room is like from the floor, and from behind the kit, written by a drummer who has loaded in through a lot of back doors.

By Miki Drummer · Vancouver, BC · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

British Columbia's punk and metal scene doesn't run on one big club. It runs on a dozen rooms of every size: theatres that hold touring headliners, bars where a local band plays its first show, and halls that put on all-ages gigs. Here are the ones worth knowing, grouped by what kind of night you're after.

Every venue below links to its page on the Punk BC board, which lists the upcoming punk, hardcore and metal shows there, with tickets.

Vancouver

Rickshaw Theatre: the home of heavy music

254 E Hastings St · Upcoming shows

If you only go to one venue in BC for punk and metal, make it this one. The Rickshaw is an old movie theatre on the Downtown Eastside. The seats at the front come out for shows, so there's a pit at the bottom of a sloped floor and seats further back if you'd rather watch. It books more punk and metal than any other room in the province: international metal tours, celtic punk weekends like Punkstravaganza, and local bills, often in the same week.

From the kit: the stage is wide and the sound is reliably loud and clean. It's the room where a local opener sounds as big as the headliner.

Commodore Ballroom: the big night out

868 Granville St · Upcoming shows

A ballroom from the 1920s with a famous sprung dance floor that bounces under a crowd. This is where touring punk acts land when they're too big for the Rickshaw: Dropkick Murphys-sized bills, reunion shows and multi-band package tours. Book early. The big ones sell out.

The Cobalt: the dive

917 Main St · Upcoming shows

Small, dark, sticky and loud, which is exactly the point. The Cobalt has been a punk bar on Main Street for a long time, and it's where you'll catch touring underground bands and local punk up close. No barrier, no distance, nowhere to hide if your band is sloppy.

The WISE Hall: the community hall

1882 Adanac St · Upcoming shows

A members' hall in Grandview with a stage, a bar and a hardwood floor. It feels like a house party for a few hundred people. It's the go-to for ska, rockabilly and punk nights like X-Mas in Skaville, and the crowd actually dances.

Red Gate Arts Society: DIY on Main

1965 Main St · Upcoming shows

An artist-run space and one of the few places in the city where the DIY spirit still has a permanent address. Expect hardcore matinees, touring basement-level punk and odd, wonderful bills you won't see anywhere else.

The Astoria: Hastings heaviness

769 E Hastings St · Upcoming shows

A long-running East Van bar with a stage at the back that leans hard toward black metal, death metal and punk. It's cheap, close and loud. A good place to find the next band you'll be telling people about.

The Pearl, Biltmore Cabaret and LanaLou's

The Pearl (881 Granville St) · Biltmore Cabaret (2755 Prince Edward St) · LanaLou's (362 Powell St)

Three mid-sized and small rooms that take a lot of the punk and metal overflow. The Pearl picks up touring metalcore and street punk. The Biltmore is a classic Mount Pleasant club for up-and-coming touring bands. LanaLou's puts on punk nights like the Angry Snowmans' holiday show.

Green Auto: DIY in East Van

1822 Pandora St · Upcoming shows

An East Van DIY space for local punk bills and touring underground acts. It's raw and affordable, and it's where a lot of the city's newer bands are cutting their teeth.

PNE Forum: arena-sized

2901 E Hastings St · Upcoming shows

An old hockey barn at the PNE grounds that turns into Vancouver's biggest general-admission room for heavy music. This is where the arena-level metal and punk tours stop, with a huge floor and a pit to match.

Metro Vancouver

Bully's Studios, New Westminster

Upcoming shows

A small, very-much-a-scene room just outside the city. It's worth the SkyTrain ride for touring underground bills like the Lethbridge Invasion.

Victoria

Lucky Bar

517 Yates St · Victoria shows

Victoria's main room for punk, garage and touring underground bands. A downtown bar on Yates Street that has seen a lot of sweaty nights.

Capital Ballroom

858 Yates St · Victoria shows

The island's bigger stage, where touring acts play when they come over on the ferry. If a band you love is doing a full BC run, this is usually the Victoria date.

Elsewhere in BC

Touring punk bands don't stop at the coast. Kelowna, Nanaimo and other towns host shows in breweries, bars and community halls, often on the routing between Vancouver and the Prairies. The Restarts' latest run, for example, hit a Kelowna brewery and Nanaimo's Globe on the way through. The Punk BC board lists these whenever they're announced.

Tips for a better night

  • Bring earplugs. Every room on this list is loud enough to leave your ears ringing for days. Drummers learn this the hard way.
  • Check the age limit. Most bars are 19+. All-ages shows are rarer and usually at halls and DIY spaces.
  • Buy from the venue or the band. Many smaller shows sell tickets at the door or through the venue's own site, which cuts out reseller fees.
  • Get there for the openers. The local bands opening tonight are the ones headlining these rooms in a few years.
