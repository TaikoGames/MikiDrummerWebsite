Where the notes come from

Nobody charted these songs by hand. A script listens to each recording and looks for the moments a sound starts — not just energy going up, which in a punk mix happens on every strum, but the sharp arrival that a stick or a beater makes. Then it finds the song's pulse, lays the actual beats down, and keeps only the hits that land on them. Whichever part of the spectrum jumped hardest decides the lane: the bottom is the kick, the crack around 1.5 kHz to 5 kHz is the snare, above 7 kHz is the cymbals. So the notes coming down the lanes are the parts Miki actually played, not somebody's transcription of them — and the background pulses on the beats the tracker found, which is why it is in time.

It is not a perfect listener and it is not pretending to be. A bass guitar sits close enough to a kick drum to get a vote, a guitar chord in the snare's range sometimes gets charted as a snare, and two of these are mixes whose low end is squashed flat enough that the kick is barely there to find — those charts are honestly thin on kicks rather than dishonestly full of invented ones. Twelve songs so far, across Bound By None, Dead Fast, Diera, Lift the Anchor and Granite. Adding another is one workflow run.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. No account, no download, no ads, nothing to install. It is a web page.

Can I play a drum game on my phone?

Yes — tap the three pads along the bottom. Turn the phone sideways and they get bigger. It works in any current mobile browser.

Do I need a real drum kit or an electronic kit?

No. It is three keys on a keyboard (A, S and space) or three pads on a touchscreen. If you do own a kit, this is not a replacement for a practice pad — it is a reading and timing exercise you can do on a bus.

Where do the drum charts come from?

They are detected from the recordings. A script finds the moment each drum is struck, works out the song's tempo, tracks the beats, and keeps the hits that land on them. Nobody transcribed these by hand, so the notes are the parts that were actually played rather than somebody's approximation of them.

The notes feel early or late. How do I fix it?

Use the calibration on this page: it plays a steady click and measures how far off your taps land, then sets the offset for you. Bluetooth headphones are almost always the cause — they can run 150 ms or more behind the screen, which is wider than the scoring window.

Can I add my own band's songs?

Not through the page — the charting runs on a server. But the tool that does it is open in the repository, and if you are a BC band who would like a track charted, say so through the services page.
